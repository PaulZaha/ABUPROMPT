
The initial process model shows redundancy and potential inefficiencies that contribute to longer execution times and higher execution costs. By analyzing the subprocesses and their dependencies, several potential improvements can be targeted to streamline the flow and eliminate redundant actions:

1. **Parallelization of Activities**: Subprocesses that are not strictly dependent on each other, such as `w_handle_leads` and `w_complete_application`, can be executed concurrently. This parallel execution reduces the overall process time by allowing simultaneous handling rather than sequential.

2. **Minimizing Use of Optional Paths**: The xor gates used in subprocesses like `subprocess_9` and `subprocess_7` introduce optional paths that can delay decision-making and execution. By making activities such as `A_Complete` and `A_Submitted` mandatory, instead of optional, the process becomes more direct and consistent, reducing time spent on decision points.

3. **Reducing Excessive Looping**: Activities `w_handle_leads`, `w_complete_application`, and `w_call_after_offers` are in loops that allow indefinite repetition. Limiting these loops or setting completion criteria will reduce unnecessary repetitions and focus resources on progressing the application, resulting in reduced costs and time.

4. **Streamlining Cancellation Handling**: Moving the cancellation logic to an earlier step in the process can prevent unnecessary activities and resources from being allocated, thus saving both time and cost.

5. **Optimization of Offer Creation**: Combining the offer creation tasks within a single subprocess without looping or optional paths will expedite the offer generation and mailing process, reducing bottlenecks and waiting times.

Overall, these improvements streamline execution and focus resources more effectively, reducing both execution time and costs.
