 The process model can be improved by optimizing the utilization of lab tests and IV treatments, which are the major cost drivers in the emergency room treatment process. The lab tests (Leucocytes, CRP, and LacticAcid) and the IV treatments (IV Liquid and IV Antibiotics) should be standardized based on clinical protocols to reduce unnecessary testing and treatment application when not indicated by the initial triage or sepsis triage. I propose the following improvements:

1. Change the subprocess_2 to execute CRP only if Leukocytes indicate a potential sepsis risk, thereby reducing unnecessary CRP testing costs.
2. Change subprocess_3 to execute IV Liquid only if Lactic Acid indicates potential dehydration or if sepsis triage requires it, thus minimizing unnecessary IV costs.

These changes are feasible given the typical protocol-based decision-making in ER settings and contribute to the improvement goal by reducing process execution cost without compromising patient care quality.
