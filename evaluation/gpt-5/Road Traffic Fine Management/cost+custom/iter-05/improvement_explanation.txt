Key issues in the original model that drive cost and false fines: (1) fines can be sent without robust pre-send validation; (2) penalty may be added immediately after sending, regardless of payment status; (3) payment is modeled as a 1..n loop, inflating handling effort; (4) escalation to collections can happen without a last pre-collection check; and (5) channel choice for sending fines is not optimized for cost. Targeted improvements: 1) Pre-send validation and stop path to reduce false fines: Introduced a validation chain (Auto-Validate Fine Data -> Check Exemptions and Duplicates -> Validate Owner and Address) and an optional Manual Review (If Flagged). After these, an explicit decision XOR either Cancels the Fine (invalid) or proceeds to sending. This directly reduces falsely sent fines and downstream rework costs. 2) Cost-optimized sending: Added XOR between Send Fine (Digital) and Send Fine (Postal) so the process can prefer digital delivery to avoid printing/postage costs. 3) Corrected escalation logic: After sending, the process must either receive Payment or follow a structured escalation: Insert Fine Notification -> Add penalty -> XOR between Payment or Pre-Collection Validation -> Send for Credit Collection. This prevents premature penalties and reduces both false penalties and unnecessary collection actions. 4) Removed multi-payment loop: Replaced the unbounded Payment loop with single-payment options (before and after penalty), lowering repeated handling costs and ambiguity. 5) Pre-collection validation: An explicit Pre-Collection Validation step is required before Send for Credit Collection, reducing wrongly escalated cases and associated fees. The resulting model reduces execution cost by avoiding unnecessary mailings, loops, and mistaken escalations, and reduces falsely sent fines by gating send with automated and manual checks and providing a Cancel Fine path.
