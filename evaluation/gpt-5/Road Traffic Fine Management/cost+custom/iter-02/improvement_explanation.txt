Key issues in the original model that drive cost and false fines: (1) Send Fine was optional, so cases could be sent to credit collection or even reach a forced Payment loop without ever sending the fine. (2) There was no upfront validation, so incorrect/duplicate fines could be issued. (3) Escalations to penalty and credit collection could occur without explicit gating checks, risking action on already-paid or wrongly issued fines. (4) A payment self-loop enforced at least one payment if that branch was chosen, which can be unrealistic and can model undesirable behavior. What changed and why this improves outcomes: 1) Validation gate before issuing a fine: Create Fine -> Validate Fine Data -> XOR(Cancel Fine, Proceed). Invalid fines are canceled early, preventing mistakenly sent fines and avoiding high downstream costs (mailing, reminders, disputes, collections). 2) Send Fine is now mandatory if the case proceeds: Once validation passes, Send Fine is executed; there is no path that skips sending and still escalates. This removes the prior defect that enabled sending to credit collection without notification. 3) Explicit payment-versus-overdue decision after Send Fine: The proceed path uses XOR between: (a) a payment branch with at-least-one payment followed by Close Case, and (b) an overdue branch that waits until the due date and then checks status before any escalation. This concentrates payment handling and stops processing as soon as paid, cutting execution cost. 4) Gated and staged escalation with checks: The overdue branch enforces Wait for Due Date -> Check Payment Status -> XOR(Already paid -> Close Case, Not paid -> Reminder). After the reminder it again waits a grace period and checks status before applying penalty and sending to credit collection. This prevents penalties and collections when payment has arrived, thereby reducing falsely sent fines and expensive rework and fees. 5) Close Case termination points: After payment or a positive status check, Close Case stops all further activities, reducing unnecessary work and cost. 6) Safe reuse via copies: Close Case is reused via .copy() where needed in XOR branches to preserve model correctness. Result: The new structure systematically prevents escalation on unissued or invalid fines and halts processing immediately when payment is detected. It reduces expensive, error-prone activities (penalties, collections, complaint handling) and mailing costs, directly supporting the goals of lowering execution cost and reducing falsely sent fines.
