Key changes to reduce end-to-end execution time: 1) Parallelize work after fine creation: Send Fine and Insert Fine Notification now start immediately and run in parallel with the resolution path (payment/escalation), removing the previous sequential gating that delayed payment until after sending. 2) Immediate payment attempt: The first Payment attempt starts right after Create Fine. This is modeled with a loop where Payment is the do-part and Add penalty is the redo-part; penalties are only applied if another attempt is needed, avoiding unnecessary penalty work before a first attempt and reducing time to closure when the first payment succeeds. 3) Eliminate optional do-nothing branches: Removed skip options that could leave the process idle and delay resolution. The process now deterministically proceeds to resolution (pay or escalate). 4) Simplify and flatten structure: Removed nested partial orders and combined dependencies into a single top-level partial order to avoid implicit sequencing and to maximize concurrency. Overall, these changes reduce waiting by enabling early payment and removing unnecessary sequencing and options that prolonged cycle times, while keeping the model operationally feasible (asynchronous sending, online payment, and rapid escalation policy).
