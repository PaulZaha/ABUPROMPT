To reduce falsely sent fines, the redesigned process introduces mandatory pre-send validation and approval, a controlled decision point, and corrected downstream dependencies so that no collection or penalty can occur without a properly approved and sent fine.
Key improvements:
1) Pre-send validation and approval gate: Added four validations executed in parallel after fine creation: Validate Recipient Data (ensures correct person/address), Verify Violation Evidence (ensures the violation is legitimate and supported), Duplicate Check (prevents duplicate fines), and Check Waiver/Prior Payment (prevents sending fines to exempt or already-settled cases). All four validations must complete before Approve Fine to Send can occur, creating a robust gate that blocks sending unless the case is clean.
2) Correction loop before approval: Introduced Correct Fine Data as a redo step via a loop. If validations fail or issues are found, the process can iterate corrections and re-run validations before any send decision is made. This reduces false fines caused by data errors or missing evidence.
3) Explicit decision to send or cancel: Replaced the optional send with an explicit XOR between sending and canceling the fine, only reachable after the validation-and-approval loop. This ensures sending is a deliberate, controlled outcome and provides a clear cancel path when the fine is invalid.
4) Downstream safeguards: Reordered post-send steps so Insert Fine Notification is created only after Send Fine, and ensured Payment and any escalation (Add penalty -> Send for Credit Collection) happen only after Send Fine. This prevents collection actions for fines that were not sent or were canceled.
5) Payment loop refined: Modeled Payment as a 0..n self-loop to reflect real behavior (payments may be zero or multiple partial payments) without impacting the false-send objective, and placed escalation after payment attempts.
Collectively, these changes add strong validation, approval, and rework controls before sending, and eliminate pathways that previously allowed collection without a sent fine—directly reducing the incidence of falsely sent fines.
