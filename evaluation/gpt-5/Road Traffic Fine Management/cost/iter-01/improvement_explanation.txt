Primary cost drivers in the original model were: (1) an unbounded payment loop that can trigger unlimited internal processing and reconciliation, (2) an early/parallel option to outsource to credit collection (an expensive third party) even before attempting internal collection, and (3) penalty/notification work potentially executed even for cases that would pay quickly. Additionally, the model allowed skipping essential steps (e.g., sending the fine), which creates exceptions that are costly to fix.

What changed and why it reduces cost:
- Limited payment retries to a maximum of two attempts: replaced the unbounded self-loop on Payment with an XOR that allows either one payment attempt or a sequence of two attempts (using copy to model the second attempt). This caps internal handling/reconciliation effort and payment-provider fees while maintaining a reasonable chance of self-resolution.
- Gated expensive outsourcing behind internal attempts: Send for Credit Collection is now only reachable in an optional escalation branch that comes after internal payment attempts. This avoids premature outsourcing and the associated third‑party costs.
- Bundle penalty-related work into escalation only: Insert Fine Notification and Add penalty are executed together just before sending to Credit Collection, preventing unnecessary penalty/notification work (and reversals) for payers who settle in the internal phase.
- Removed do-nothing/skip variants that caused exceptions: Send Fine is now mandatory after Create Fine, and the terminal None-choice that could end the case without action is eliminated. This reduces exception handling and rework costs.

Resulting flow: Create Fine -> Send Fine -> Payment (1x or 2x) -> optionally escalate (Insert Fine Notification -> Add penalty -> Send for Credit Collection). The redesign is feasible as a policy change and configuration in existing systems, requires no new activities, and directly targets execution cost by minimizing rework, capping retries, and deferring (and often avoiding) costly outsourcing.
