Key issues in the original model that increase time and cost: 1) Send Fine was optional, allowing payment or credit collection without notifying the debtor, causing rework and delays. 2) Payment was modeled as a self-loop without reminders or penalties, leading to inefficient repeated attempts. 3) Credit collection could be chosen early, incurring third-party costs before reasonable self-service attempts. 4) Penalty and notification were positioned immediately after sending the fine, rather than being triggered only when payment was not received. Improvements made and their impact: - Make Send Fine mandatory after Create Fine, ensuring the debtor is informed before any downstream actions. This removes rework and prevents premature escalation. - Introduce a purposeful payment cycle: loop(do=Payment, redo=Add penalty then optionally Insert Fine Notification). Each redo represents a missed payment deadline, automatically applying the penalty and optionally sending a notification/reminder before allowing another payment attempt. This bundles work, reduces manual touches, and shortens cycle time. - Defer Send for Credit Collection to after the payment cycle using xor(None, Send for Credit Collection). This ensures external collection is only triggered when internal recovery efforts have been attempted, lowering fees and improving first-time payment rate. - Eliminate unnecessary branching and optionalities, streamlining the path: Create Fine -> Send Fine -> Payment cycle -> optional escalation. Result: Faster resolution (payment enabled immediately, fewer decision points), fewer communications (notification optional and only after a missed payment), and reduced external collection costs (escalation only after internal attempts). These changes are feasible with standard policy updates and automation of reminders/penalties.
