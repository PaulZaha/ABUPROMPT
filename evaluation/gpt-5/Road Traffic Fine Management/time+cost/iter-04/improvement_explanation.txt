Key improvements and their impact on time and cost:
1) Make sending the fine and inserting the notification mandatory and parallel. In the original model, both were optional and sequenced, which introduces delays and rework. By starting both immediately after fine creation and running them concurrently, we cut waiting time and reduce handoffs.
2) Remove the payment retry loop. The previous self-loop allowed repeated payment attempts, which prolongs cycle time and adds administrative costs. We replace it with a single Payment activity. Any user-side retries are handled by the payment gateway, not by the business process.
3) Apply penalties only when payment does not occur and pair it with credit collection. Previously, adding a penalty could happen right after inserting a notification, even if the debtor pays promptly. Moving Add penalty into the non-payment escalation path avoids unnecessary penalty handling and reduces processing cost.
4) Eliminate the "skip" branch after sending the fine. The original XOR included a skip option, which could leave cases unresolved. Replacing it with a clean choice (Payment vs. Escalate with penalty and credit collection) shortens resolution time and streamlines decisions.
Feasibility: Insert Fine Notification is an internal, automatable step and can safely run in parallel with sending the fine. Payment is captured once at process level, while any retries are handled by the payment system. Penalty application and credit collection naturally belong to the non-payment branch. This redesign reduces touches, avoids rework, and accelerates end-to-end throughput, directly supporting the goals of reducing execution time and cost.
