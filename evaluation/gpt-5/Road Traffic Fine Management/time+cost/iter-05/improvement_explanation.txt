Key time and cost drivers were (1) an unbounded payment self-loop that could keep cases open indefinitely, (2) a do-nothing skip path after sending the fine that allowed cases to end without resolution, and (3) administrative steps blocking progression to resolution. The redesigned model delivers the following improvements: 1) Bound payment attempts to at most two tries, then automatically escalate to credit collection. This replaces the unbounded loop with a short, deterministic path. Operationally, escalation is triggered only if the balance remains unpaid; the control-flow models this by sequencing payment attempts followed by escalation. This reduces cycle time and the cost of repeated interactions. 2) Remove the do-nothing skip option after sending the fine. The process now always proceeds to either payment attempts (then escalation) or immediate credit collection for high-risk cases. This eliminates idle terminations and accelerates closure. 3) Start resolution (payment attempts or immediate collection) immediately after sending the fine, while administrative tasks (Insert Fine Notification → Add penalty) run in parallel. Previously, the resolution path waited behind optional admin work; now it is decoupled to shorten throughput time. 4) Keep notification/penalty optional to avoid unnecessary work if the case resolves quickly, but allow it to run concurrently to minimize overall duration. Collectively, these changes reduce process execution time by eliminating waits and unbounded cycles, and reduce cost by curbing rework and repeated manual efforts, while keeping the model feasible and implementable with standard policy rules and automation.
