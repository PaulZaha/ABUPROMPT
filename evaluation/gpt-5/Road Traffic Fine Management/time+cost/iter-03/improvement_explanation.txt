Key improvements and their impact on time and cost: 1) Removed the skip-at-end behavior: previously, after creating a fine, the process could entirely skip payment and collection, leading to incomplete cases and later rework. The new decision phase forces a clear outcome: either a payment attempt or an escalation, reducing idle time and coordination overhead. 2) Limited payment retries: replaced the unbounded payment self-loop with up to two attempts (second attempt optional). This curbs repeated manual follow-ups and system retries, cutting both cycle time and processing cost. 3) Moved penalty to the escalation path only: penalty is now applied only when payment is not pursued or fails and the case moves to collection, avoiding unnecessary penalty work on successfully paid cases. 4) Parallelized non-critical notification: the “Insert Fine Notification” step is optional and can run in parallel with downstream decisioning, so it no longer gates payment or escalation, shortening the overall duration. 5) Simplified control flow: flattened into a single partial order for the main orchestration, reducing complexity and hidden dependencies. Feasibility: As process owner, enforcing a maximum of two payment attempts is a realistic policy change, making the process predictable. Making the notification optional and non-blocking reflects practical variability in regulatory requirements while avoiding delays. Mandatory sending of the fine ensures customers receive the claim before any downstream actions, improving first-time resolution and reducing costly escalations.
