Main cost drivers in the original model are the unbounded self-loops on manual tasks (W_Handle leads, W_Complete application, W_Call after offers) and two nested loop constructs that can create multiple rework cycles. Repeated human touches are typically the largest unit cost, so removing or time-boxing these loops reduces execution cost without removing essential control points. Improvements applied: 1) Eliminated unbounded rework on manual tasks. Replaced loop(W_Handle leads, None) and loop(W_Call after offers, None) with xor(None, ...) so they can occur at most once when needed. Replaced loop(W_Complete application, None) with a single execution to standardize work to one complete pass. This directly reduces repeated labor. 2) Removed nested do–redo loops. Flattened subprocess_12 and subprocess_11 into a straight-through sequence. This prevents ping-ponging between calls and end-state decisions, cutting rework and handoffs. 3) Kept value-optional steps optional. A_Concept and the offer chain remain optional, so we don’t incur the cost when they are not required. 4) Streamlined end-state decision. A single xor between A_Complete and the cancellation chain (A_Cancelled -> O_Cancelled) ensures a clean, single decision with no loops. 5) Made A_Submitted a mandatory gate right after creation to ensure early validation and reduce downstream rework. Expected impact: fewer manual iterations on leads, application completion, and follow-up calls; fewer context switches; and a lower average number of human touches per case. All original activities are preserved, ordering is clarified, and optionality is retained where it avoids unnecessary cost.
