Main time bottlenecks in the as-is model are two unnecessary serializations: (1) Receive Order Confirmation gates the start of downstream execution, and (2) the entire invoice-handling chain is forced to start only after the goods-receipt/vendor-invoice-creation branch completes. In practice, these activities belong to different teams and can proceed in parallel once the PO item is created. To reduce process execution time, I removed the superfluous ordering constraints and fanned out three concurrent branches directly from Create Purchase Order Item: (a) optional Receive Order Confirmation, (b) concurrent Record Goods Receipt and optional Vendor creates invoice, and (c) the optional invoice-handling chain (Record Invoice Receipt -> optional Remove Payment Block -> Clear Invoice). This decoupling shortens end-to-end lead time by eliminating idle waits: AP can record/clear invoices as soon as they arrive, warehouse can record goods receipt without waiting for confirmation, and procurement can process confirmations in parallel. The improvements preserve feasibility: all previously optional steps remain optional, and the internal sequencing within the invoice chain (invoice receipt before optional block before clearing) is maintained. This redesign exploits safe parallelism across procurement, warehouse, and AP to achieve shorter execution time without adding work.
