Key time-reduction changes and why they work:
- Decoupled “Receive Order Confirmation” from the critical path: Previously, Goods Receipt and the whole invoice stream waited for the optional Order Confirmation. Making Order Confirmation optional and concurrent with downstream steps removes avoidable waiting time.
- Enabled early invoice processing: Instead of forcing invoice tasks to wait for Goods Receipt, the invoice subflow now starts right after PO creation. Vendor creates invoice -> Record Invoice Receipt -> (optional) Remove Payment Block -> Clear Invoice are sequenced inside the subflow. This shortens the invoice cycle and overall lead time.
- Straight-through clearing when there’s no block: Clear Invoice now follows directly after Invoice Receipt when no payment block is raised; if a block exists, it must be removed first. This supports straight-through processing and reduces end-to-end time.
- Flattened unnecessary ordering constraints: The previous model used nested partial orders that imposed extra sequencing (e.g., requiring Subprocess_3 before the invoice handling). The improved model keeps one top-level partial order with concurrent branches after PO creation, minimizing artificial waits while keeping optionality via XOR.
- Feasibility: These changes reflect common P2P best practices. Order Confirmation is informational and shouldn’t delay GR or invoicing. Early invoice capture and conditional (two-way) clearing are standard for low-risk/low-value items, with payment-block removal providing a robust control mechanism when mismatches occur.
Overall impact: The critical path is shortened by (1) starting invoice activities earlier, (2) eliminating the wait for Order Confirmation before GR and invoicing, and (3) allowing automatic clearing when no block is present. This directly reduces average process execution time without adding rework loops or complexity.
