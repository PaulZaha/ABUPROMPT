Key change: maximize parallelism while preserving control points required for compliance. In the original model, invoice activities were gated behind goods receipt via a sequential chain of sub-blocks (Create PO -> optional Order Confirmation -> [GR and Vendor Invoice Creation in parallel] -> optional Invoice Receipt/Payment chain). This delayed invoice posting and payment preparation until after logistics steps finished, increasing overall cycle time. Improvements made: 1) Decouple and parallelize non-critical steps: Receive Order Confirmation is modeled as an optional, fully parallel step after PO creation so it never blocks logistics or finance activities. Vendor creates invoice remains optional and parallel. 2) Overlap invoice processing with logistics: Record Invoice Receipt can start immediately after PO creation. Remove Payment Block (if any) follows invoice receipt. 3) Preserve key control while enabling speed: Clear Invoice now depends on two prerequisites: (a) completion of the invoice path (record invoice receipt and any payment block handling) and (b) completion of goods receipt. This ensures no payment is cleared before goods receipt, while allowing invoice verification and block removal to occur earlier. 4) Flatten nested structures into a single partial order to remove unnecessary sequencing barriers and expose the maximum concurrency safely. Expected effect: invoice verification can be completed in parallel with receiving goods; once goods receipt is posted, the invoice can be cleared immediately if matched and unblocked. Removing the order confirmation gate and enabling early invoice posting reduces the critical path and thus the end-to-end execution time.
