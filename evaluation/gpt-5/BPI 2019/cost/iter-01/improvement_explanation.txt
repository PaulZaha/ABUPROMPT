To reduce execution cost, the process is redesigned to maximize straight-through processing (STP) and minimize manual touches, while keeping manual steps as exception-only fallbacks. Key changes: 1) Introduced a settlement-mode XOR with three branches: ERS (self-billing), EDI STP, and Manual. ERS eliminates vendor invoices and internal invoice receipt/clear entirely, removing multiple manual tasks. EDI STP consolidates invoice posting and clearing into one automated step, significantly reducing clerk effort. Manual processing is retained only for exceptions. 2) Restricted Remove Payment Block to the manual branch and kept it optional; this prevents performing it unless actually needed. 3) Strengthened dependencies in the manual branch so Clear Invoice occurs only after both Goods Receipt and Invoice Receipt (and, if present, the Payment Block removal). This reduces exception-handling and rework costs due to premature clearing. 4) Moved vendor-created invoice exclusively into invoice-based branches so it is not (implicitly) considered in ERS scenarios, which avoids unnecessary interactions and associated costs. 5) Made order confirmation low-touch: added an Auto-Process Order Confirmation option and kept confirmation optional/exception-based, reducing manual handling for standard cases. 6) Preserved beneficial concurrency where appropriate (invoice creation can occur in parallel with goods receipt in invoice-based branches) to avoid wait-induced handling and coordination overhead. Overall, the redesign pushes the majority of cases through zero- or one-touch automated paths (ERS/EDI), with manual steps isolated to exceptions, delivering lower per-case processing cost.
