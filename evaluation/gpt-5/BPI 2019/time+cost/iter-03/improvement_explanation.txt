Key issues identified in the original model that increase time and cost: (1) The invoice-processing segment is gated by the partial order subprocess_3, which forces completion of both goods receipt and the optional vendor invoice creation before invoice work can even start. This delays recording the invoice and downstream clearing. (2) The goods receipt is unnecessarily gated by the optional order confirmation via (subprocess_0, subprocess_3). (3) Nested partial orders obscure concurrency and create rigidity. (4) An external step (Vendor creates invoice) is modeled as part of our process, adding artificial waiting without any actionable work on our side. Improvements implemented: - Flattened the structure into a single partial_order, avoiding partial orders as children and explicitly encoding all dependencies. This makes concurrency explicit and prevents hidden sequencing constraints. - Removed the external “Vendor creates invoice” activity and the top-level optionality of the entire invoice-processing path. The process now focuses only on our internal activities. - Enabled true parallelization after PO creation: Receive Order Confirmation (optional), Record Goods Receipt, and Record Invoice Receipt can proceed independently as soon as the PO item is created. This removes unnecessary waiting and shortens cycle time. - Enforced a clean 3-way-match for payment: Clear Invoice is now contingent on both Record Goods Receipt and Record Invoice Receipt, and (if present) Remove Payment Block. Remove Payment Block remains optional and only executes when needed, thereby saving effort and cost for the majority of invoices that pass automated checks. How these changes reduce time and cost: - Parallel execution removes idle time: invoice receipt and goods receipt can be captured as soon as they occur, without waiting for order confirmation or for the vendor-side step. - Elimination of non-value-added steps and artificial gates reduces handoffs and delays. - Conditional handling of payment blocks avoids unnecessary work on clear invoices, cutting effort and cycle time. These adjustments are standard and feasible in a procure-to-pay context (e.g., with e-invoicing and automated match rules), ensuring faster throughput and lower operational cost while preserving control.
