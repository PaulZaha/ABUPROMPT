Key bottlenecks in the original model were (1) the strict sequencing that made downstream activities wait for receiving the order confirmation and goods receipt, (2) placing invoice recording strictly after goods receipt, which delays matching and exception discovery, and (3) an optional skip of the entire invoice processing branch, which is not realistic and encourages variance and rework. To reduce execution time and cost, the redesigned model: (a) starts three parallel flows immediately after the purchase order creation—receiving order confirmation (optional), recording goods receipt, and vendor invoice creation (optional)—so no downstream task is gated by the order confirmation; (b) allows the recording of invoice receipt as soon as the vendor invoice is available (concurrent with goods receipt), which pulls exception detection forward; (c) models straight-through processing (STP) by keeping a manual Remove Payment Block step optional via XOR, only requiring it when a match fails; and (d) ensures compliance by requiring Clear Invoice to wait for both invoice receipt and goods receipt, preventing premature payment and avoiding rework. The model now uses a single partial order with precise dependencies to maximize concurrency while preserving necessary controls. This reduces waiting, shortens the end-to-end cycle, cuts manual touches, and helps avoid exception-driven rework—lowering both time and cost.
