Key issues found: (1) The treatment block is optional for every patient after sepsis triage, which can cause both undertreatment and overtreatment. (2) Antibiotics were gated only by IV fluids, not by diagnostic confirmation or safeguards (allergy check), which increases wrong-treatment risk. (3) Lactic acid was optional while it is critical for sepsis severity assessment; CRP was always ordered, raising cost. (4) A partial order was nested within another partial order, which can hide dependencies and makes correctness harder to enforce. Improvements to reduce cost and decrease wrong treatments: - Clear decision after ER Sepsis Triage: use an exclusive choice between doing the sepsis workup/treatment path and skipping it for non-septic patients (explicit XOR). - Mandatory key diagnostics; rationalize labs: LacticAcid is made mandatory in the sepsis path (reduces misclassification and wrong therapy), while CRP becomes optional to reduce lab cost when not clinically needed. Leucocytes remains mandatory. All labs run in parallel to avoid delays. - Safety gates for antibiotics: add Allergy Check and Antibiotic Approval. Antibiotic Approval is constrained to occur after Leucocytes and LacticAcid are available; IV Antibiotics cannot be executed unless both Allergy Check and Antibiotic Approval have been completed. This directly decreases wrong antibiotics. - Make IV Antibiotics optional behind approval: antibiotics may be skipped when clinical review (approval) determines they are unnecessary, lowering both wrong treatments and costs. - Make IV Liquid optional and independent: fluids can be administered when indicated without being a hard prerequisite for antibiotics, reducing unnecessary fluid administration (cost and clinical risk). - Concurrency preserved and partial orders flattened: labs, fluids, and checks can occur in parallel where safe, keeping cycle times low while enforcing critical dependencies; no partial order is nested inside another partial order. Expected impact: fewer unnecessary CRP orders, fewer unnecessary fluids/antibiotics via approval gate, and fewer adverse events from allergic reactions or premature antibiotics; overall cost reduction and fewer wrong treatments without delaying necessary care.
