Key improvements and how they meet the goals: 1) Add Antibiotic Indication Check after labs and before any treatment. This decision step prevents administering antibiotics when sepsis is unlikely, directly reducing wrong treatments and avoiding the significant costs of unnecessary antibiotics and related adverse events. 2) Introduce Antibiotic Allergy Check before IV Antibiotics. This minimizes harm from allergy-related errors and avoids the downstream costs associated with adverse drug reactions, while adding negligible process cost. 3) Move LacticAcid to the treatment-indicated branch and keep it optional. Lactate is no longer routinely ordered for low-risk cases, reducing test costs while keeping it available for high-risk/severe cases. 4) Preserve parallel collection of Leucocytes and CRP to minimize wait time without adding cost and to enable a timely, informed decision. 5) Replace the prior "silent optional treatment" with an explicit XOR branch after ER Sepsis Triage: either skip sepsis management (no suspicion) or proceed with labs and the indication check. This removes unnecessary downstream actions for non-suspected cases, reducing cost. 6) Enforce safe sequencing: labs -> indication check -> (if indicated) IV Liquid -> Antibiotic Allergy Check -> IV Antibiotics, with LacticAcid optionally before antibiotics. This sequencing aligns with clinical best practices, protecting against wrong treatments while avoiding extraneous steps. Together, these changes reduce the frequency of unnecessary lactate tests and antibiotic administrations, cutting execution costs and decreasing wrong treatments via explicit decision gates and an allergy safety check.
