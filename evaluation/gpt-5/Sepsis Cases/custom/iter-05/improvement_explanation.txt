Targeted changes to reduce wrong treatments focus on adding explicit decision gates, mandatory diagnostic steps for suspected sepsis, and safety checks before administering therapy. Key improvements: 1) Introduced an exclusive choice after ER Sepsis Triage between a suspected-sepsis path and an observation path to avoid unnecessary antibiotics in low-risk patients. 2) Made Lactate measurement mandatory in the suspected-sepsis path and added an ‘Interpret Sepsis Labs’ activity, ensuring treatment decisions are based on objective diagnostics. 3) Added pre-antibiotic safeguards: ‘Allergy & Contraindication Check’, guideline-based ‘Antibiotic Selection’, ‘Dose Calculation & Verification’, and a ‘Second Clinician Double-Check’, plus mandatory ‘Blood Cultures’ before antibiotic administration. These steps directly mitigate wrong drug choice, dose errors, and contraindications. 4) Made IV fluids optional and gated by a ‘Fluid Responsiveness Assessment’ to prevent inappropriate fluid administration. Structurally, labs run concurrently and must precede interpretation; treatment only begins after labs and interpretation, with safety checks enforced via partial order dependencies; a dedicated observation path keeps non-septic patients from receiving antibiotics. All changes are feasible within ER workflows (nurse/pharmacist double-check, standard order sets, culture collection before antibiotics) and directly lower the risk of wrong treatments by tightening clinical decision-making and medication safety control points.
