Key issues found in the original model that cause wrong treatments: (1) All post-triage activities are optional, so patients can receive no diagnostics or therapy after ER Sepsis Triage, increasing omission errors; (2) Lactic acid is optional, though it is critical for sepsis severity assessment; (3) Antibiotics can be given without safety checks (allergy, dosing) and without obtaining cultures first, which is a common cause of wrong or suboptimal therapy; (4) IV fluids are mandated before antibiotics with no contraindication check, risking harm in CHF/renal failure patients; (5) No explicit split between sepsis vs. non-sepsis after triage, so non-septic patients might inadvertently receive antibiotics.

Targeted redesign to reduce wrong treatments:
- Explicit decision split after ER Sepsis Triage: XOR between Sepsis suspected (protocol path) and Non-sepsis care. This minimizes inappropriate antibiotics in non-septic patients and avoids therapeutic omissions by clarifying the intended path.
- Mandatory and concurrent diagnostic bundle in the sepsis path: Leucocytes, CRP, Lactic Acid, and Blood Cultures are all triggered by Activate Sepsis Protocol. Cultures are explicitly constrained to precede IV Antibiotics, preventing the common error of administering antibiotics before cultures.
- Pre-antibiotic safeguards: Antibiotic Indication Check and Allergy Check precede Antibiotic Order & Dose, followed by Pharmacist Verification before IV Antibiotics. This sequence reduces wrong drug selection, dosing errors, and allergy-related adverse events.
- Safe fluid administration: An XOR branch enforces either a Fluid Contraindication Check followed by IV Liquid, or documentation of a contraindication. This prevents wrong fluid treatment in high-risk patients while ensuring documentation when fluids are withheld.
- Reassessment loop after initial antibiotics: a self-loop for Reassess Patient and Adjust Therapy enables iterative correction if initial therapy was suboptimal or wrong, reducing sustained treatment errors as data return.

Feasibility: Steps align with standard ED and Surviving Sepsis Campaign practices. Concurrency is used to prevent delays (labs and checks run in parallel), while key dependency constraints (cultures before antibiotics; checks before orders) enforce safety without adding unnecessary bottlenecks.
