Targeted improvements and how they meet the goals:
- Remove unnecessary wait for labs before treatment: Previously, all labs had to finish before any treatment. We now allow labs (Leucocytes, CRP, LacticAcid) and treatments to run in parallel, which shortens the critical path and reduces overall execution time.
- Make Lactic Acid mandatory in the sepsis path: Lactate was optional; it is now required when the sepsis path is chosen, improving severity assessment and reducing wrong treatments due to under-recognition of sepsis.
- Add Blood Cultures before antibiotics: Introducing a hard dependency Blood Cultures -> IV Antibiotics ensures cultures are drawn first, improving diagnostic accuracy and limiting wrong antibiotic choices without delaying other parallel tasks.
- Add Antibiotic Allergy Check before antibiotics: A hard dependency Allergy Check -> IV Antibiotics reduces wrong treatments (e.g., allergic reactions or contraindicated agents) with negligible delay because it runs in parallel to labs and IV setup.
- Decouple IV Liquids from antibiotics: Removed the IV Liquid -> IV Antibiotics sequence. Fluids can start as soon as possible and in parallel with cultures and labs; antibiotics can start immediately after cultures and allergy check are done, cutting time-to-antibiotics.
- Preserve decision point after ER Sepsis Triage: The sepsis bundle remains under an exclusive choice after ER Sepsis Triage, reflecting that the bundle is only executed when the triage indicates sepsis, thereby avoiding overtreatment of non-sepsis patients.
- Feasibility: The model aligns with common ED sepsis protocols and EHR order sets—cultures and allergy checks are quick and can be performed during IV access preparation; fluids and labs run concurrently, while antibiotics are gated only by two fast prerequisites.
These changes reduce total elapsed time by parallelizing activities and removing nonessential sequencing, while decreasing wrong treatments through safety gates (cultures first, allergy check) and mandatory lactate measurement in the sepsis path.
