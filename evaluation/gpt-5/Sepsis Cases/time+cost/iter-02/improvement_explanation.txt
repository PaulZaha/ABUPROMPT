Key issues in the original model slowing time and adding cost: (1) All treatments wait for lab completion because of the dependency labs -> treatment; (2) IV Liquid must precede IV Antibiotics, even when both can be prepared/administered in parallel; (3) Post-triage work only starts after ER Sepsis Triage, creating idle time; (4) Nested partial orders make the structure more rigid than needed. Targeted improvements: 1) Parallelize diagnostics and stabilizing care immediately after ER Triage. Labs (Leucocytes, CRP, optional LacticAcid) and IV Liquid can start right after ER Triage, in parallel with ER Sepsis Triage. This removes idle time and reduces door-to-treatment. 2) Gate antibiotics by ER Sepsis Triage but remove unnecessary waits. IV Antibiotics become optional and can start as soon as ER Sepsis Triage is done; they no longer wait for labs or for IV Liquid to complete. This reduces time while controlling cost by avoiding antibiotics for non-sepsis cases. 3) Remove the forced sequence IV Liquid -> IV Antibiotics. Fluids and antibiotics are decoupled; fluids can start early, antibiotics start after sepsis confirmation, which shortens the critical path without extra cost. 4) Keep cost-sensitive items optional. LacticAcid remains optional inside the lab bundle; the whole lab bundle and both treatments are optional. This avoids unnecessary spend when sepsis is not suspected. 5) Simplify structure to avoid nested partial orders where not needed. The final top-level partial order orchestrates concurrency and the few essential dependencies. Expected impact: - Time reduction: Early parallel start for labs and fluids and elimination of the lab-before-treatment constraint shorten the path to first treatment and antibiotics. - Cost reduction: Optionalization of labs and antibiotics limits their use to indicated cases; lactic acid stays optional. - Feasibility: Mirrors common ER protocols (early fluids and labs upon suspicion; antibiotics after clinical confirmation), and uses standard order sets to pre-authorize early starts from triage.
