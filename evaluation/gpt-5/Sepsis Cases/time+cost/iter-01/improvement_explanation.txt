Key improvements to reduce execution time and cost while keeping clinical feasibility: 1) Start diagnostic labs in parallel with sepsis triage immediately after ER triage. This eliminates idle time waiting for triage decisions and cuts turnaround time for results. 2) Remove the dependency that labs must finish before initiating treatment; IV fluids and antibiotics can begin as soon as sepsis triage confirms suspicion. This accelerates time-to-first-dose and resuscitation. 3) Administer IV fluids and IV antibiotics concurrently instead of sequentially to shorten overall treatment time. 4) Make CRP and Leucocytes individually optional, and keep Lactic Acid optional, so that labs are ordered only when clinically indicated (driven by triage/sepsis criteria), lowering test utilization and cost. 5) Flatten the structure by avoiding a partial order nested directly inside another partial order, improving clarity and avoiding missed dependencies. Operationally, triage nurses can pre-order labs based on sepsis screening criteria; treatment begins once sepsis triage indicates need, and nursing/phlebotomy can perform lab draws and IV administration in parallel, reducing length of stay and consumable use through targeted testing.
