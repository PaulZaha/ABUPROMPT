Cost drivers in the current flow are the default ordering/execution of multiple labs (Leucocytes and CRP, plus optional LacticAcid) and the broad availability of IV therapy, even for patients who may not need it. To reduce execution cost without compromising feasibility, I restructured the post-triage segment into a risk-based exclusive choice driven by the ER Sepsis Triage outcome: (1) Low-risk: skip all labs/therapy; (2) Moderate-risk: perform only CRP (cheapest, quick indicator); (3) High-risk: run Leucocytes with optional LacticAcid and administer IV therapy (IV Liquid -> IV Antibiotics). Additional cost savings come from removing CRP from the high-risk path, which avoids redundant testing when rapid treatment is already indicated. The IV therapy is now restricted to the high-risk path, preventing unnecessary IV use in low/moderate cases. Concurrency is preserved where appropriate to avoid prolonging throughput without increasing cost. Structurally, the design lowers the average number of executed activities per patient: low-risk patients incur zero cost post-triage, moderate-risk patients incur only one lab test, and high-risk patients avoid the extra CRP previously done by default. This is feasible by embedding simple risk-based order sets into the existing Sepsis Triage decision, requiring no additional steps and aligning with standard sepsis protocols.
