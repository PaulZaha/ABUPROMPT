Cost drivers in the original model were (1) ordering CRP for all cases alongside leucocytes, (2) routinely giving both IV fluids and IV antibiotics whenever the optional treatment path was selected, and (3) running the same intensive workflow regardless of clinical risk after sepsis triage. To reduce execution cost without breaking clinical safety, the redesign introduces three targeted changes:

1) Risk-based branching after ER Sepsis Triage: A top-level xor now separates low-, moderate-, and high-risk handling. Low-risk patients can skip labs/treatment entirely. This avoids unnecessary tests and medications for a large subset of patients.

2) Stepwise, conditional lab ordering for moderate risk: Leucocytes are performed first; CRP is ordered only if clinically warranted (modeled as xor None vs CRP), and Lactate is ordered only if needed (xor None vs Lactate). This removes the default concurrent CRP testing for all patients and limits Lactate to cases with stronger suspicion, which directly reduces reagent and processing costs.

3) Granular treatment options: Instead of always administering both IV fluids and IV antibiotics whenever treatment is chosen, the treatment xor now offers three choices: skip treatment, IV fluids only, or IV fluids followed by antibiotics. This reduces antibiotic spend when clinical evidence does not justify it, while keeping the ability to escalate when needed. In the high-risk branch, labs are executed concurrently for speed, and treatment still allows fluids-only versus fluids+antibiotics, supporting cost-conscious escalation.

Implementation notes: The design avoids nesting partial orders inside other partial orders (except where a sequence is used inside an xor) and uses .copy() when the same activity appears in multiple alternative branches to keep the model well-formed. These changes are feasible operationally because they align with common sepsis protocols (risk stratification, conditional testing, and antibiotic stewardship) while delivering meaningful reductions in lab and medication costs.
