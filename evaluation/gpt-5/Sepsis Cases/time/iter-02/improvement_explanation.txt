Main bottlenecks were (a) IV therapy was forced to wait for labs to finish and (b) IV Liquid had to precede IV Antibiotics. To reduce total throughput time without changing clinical gating, I kept the whole sepsis bundle optional and only executable after ER Sepsis Triage, but restructured the internals: labs (Leucocytes, CRP, optional LacticAcid) and IV therapy now run in parallel, and within IV therapy, IV Liquid and IV Antibiotics can proceed concurrently. This shortens the critical path from labs-then-therapy to max(labs duration, therapy duration), and reduces time-to-antibiotics. LacticAcid remains optional, and IV therapy is still optional inside the bundle, preserving decision flexibility. Operationally, concurrency is feasible by enabling nurse-driven sepsis protocols, pre-authorized lab panels, and using dual-lumen/Y-site IV access to allow fluids and antibiotics concurrently.
