To reduce total execution time, I removed unnecessary sequencing and maximized parallelism around the critical early steps of suspected sepsis care while keeping clinical feasibility: 
- Start labs earlier, in parallel with ER Sepsis Triage: Triage can trigger a nurse-driven sepsis order set so blood draw (Leucocytes, CRP, optional Lactic Acid) begins immediately after ER Triage rather than waiting for ER Sepsis Triage. This cuts waiting time on lab turnaround and shortens the critical path.
- Decouple treatment from labs: IV Liquid and IV Antibiotics no longer wait for labs to complete; they can start right after ER Triage under protocolized orders. This removes a major serial dependency.
- Parallelize IV fluids and antibiotics: Change IV Liquid -> IV Antibiotics sequence to concurrent execution, assuming a second IV line or Y-connector and parallel nursing tasks. This decreases time to first antibiotic dose and overall treatment time.
- Preserve optionality to avoid unnecessary work: Lactic Acid remains optional, and both the lab block and the treatment block are optional. This prevents adding time for non-sepsis cases and focuses acceleration where it matters.
Effect on the critical path: Previously the path was Registration -> Triage -> Sepsis Triage -> Labs -> Treatment (sum of durations). Now it is Registration -> Triage -> max(Sepsis Triage, Labs, Treatment), turning several serial steps into concurrent ones and thus reducing overall throughput time. These changes are standard ED practice via standing order sets and parallel tasking, making them feasible to implement.
