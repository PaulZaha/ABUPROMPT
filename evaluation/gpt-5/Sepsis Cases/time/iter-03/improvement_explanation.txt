Key time-reduction changes applied: (1) Start diagnostics earlier and in parallel: Leucocytes, CRP, and optional Lactic Acid are moved to begin right after ER Triage, running concurrently with ER Sepsis Triage. This removes idle time and shortens the time until results are available. (2) Decouple treatment from labs: IV Antibiotics no longer waits for labs to finish; it starts immediately after ER Sepsis Triage, reducing time-to-antibiotic. (3) Remove sequential dependency between IV Liquid and IV Antibiotics: They can run in parallel instead of IV Liquid strictly preceding IV Antibiotics, cutting elapsed time for initiating therapy. (4) Allow early fluids: IV Liquid can start right after ER Triage (optional), so resuscitation does not wait for ER Sepsis Triage. (5) Preserve clinical safety and flexibility: Antibiotics still require completion of ER Sepsis Triage; Lactic Acid remains optional; and labs, fluids, and antibiotics are each independently optional, so only necessary steps are executed. Overall, these changes transform the critical path from a series of dependent steps (labs then fluids then antibiotics) into parallel streams, shifting elapsed time from a sum of durations to the maximum of overlapping durations, which directly reduces process execution time.
