What changed and why it reduces execution time: 1) Labs in parallel with Sepsis Triage: In the original model, all labs (Leucocytes, CRP, and optionally LacticAcid) had to complete before any treatment could start. We moved lab draws to begin immediately after ER Triage, in parallel with ER Sepsis Triage. This uses triage standing orders to pre-authorize lab collection for suspected sepsis, shortening the critical path by overlapping diagnostic work with clinical assessment. 2) Decoupled treatment from lab completion: IV therapy (fluids and antibiotics) no longer waits for labs. It starts as soon as Sepsis Triage confirms suspicion (clinical decision), which eliminates the labs→treatment gating and reduces time to first antibiotic dose and overall process duration. 3) Parallelized IV Liquids and IV Antibiotics: We changed their relationship from strict sequence to concurrency, reflecting feasible administration via piggyback or a second line once IV access is available. This shortens the treatment segment on the critical path. 4) Kept Lactic Acid as optional and preserved optionality of the whole treatment bundle: This respects clinical variability without adding mandatory work, focusing purely on removing unnecessary sequential constraints. Feasibility considerations: Triage standing orders for sepsis enable early labs; antibiotics can be initiated based on clinical assessment without waiting for results; and concurrent fluid/antibiotic administration is common via piggyback/second port. Net effect: The model removes two major bottlenecks (labs→treatment dependency and IV fluids→antibiotics sequence) and overlaps diagnostics and therapeutics, reducing end-to-end execution time.
